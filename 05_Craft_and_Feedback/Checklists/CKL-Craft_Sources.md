# CKL-Craft_Sources — Craft Sources and Attribution

The craft skills, checklists and style warnings of this framework are original text. They distil ideas that other people taught first. This page credits them. Nothing is copied; each source was read and restated.

## Ideas and where they come from
| Idea used | Credit |
|---|---|
| Five commandments of a scene, crisis types, value shifts | Shawn Coyne, the Story Grid method |
| Scene and sequel, goal, conflict and disaster rhythm | Dwight V. Swain, popularised for fantasy writers by Jim Butcher |
| Arc types, lie and truth, ghost, beat position windows, proof of change | K.M. Weiland, Helping Writers Become Authors |
| Laws of magic: understanding before resolution, limits over powers, expand before adding | Brandon Sanderson; Mythcreants on deus ex machina |
| Line editing: filter words, adverbs, rhythm, repetition | Louise Harnby |
| Show, subtext, exposition in small pieces | Jane Friedman and Reedsy craft articles |
| Reader simulation, review-before-refactor, failure-mode lists, state diff | Public skill collections by haowjy, Nanako0129 (sepia), danjdewhurst, howells and ThomasHoussin |
| Ideation by distinct, probability-weighted options | The creative-writing skill collections surveyed in the Area 9 research |

## How the framework uses them
- Deterministic parts (`narr stats`, STY001-STY005, FSH001-FSH003, MSC005, MSC006, CHR001) are the framework's own code.
- Judgement parts live in skills that diagnose and never rewrite prose without the curator's approval.

## Not adopted
Genre convention tables, paid-book content and any text that could not be restated were left out on purpose.
